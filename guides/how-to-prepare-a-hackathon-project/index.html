How to prepare a hackathon project

如何准备 Hackathon 作品

Short answer: Read the event rules first (eligibility, team size, what may be built before the start, judging criteria), then arrive with a problem and a team rather than finished code. During the event, scope one working demo path, keep a running README, and spend the last few hours on a short pitch that shows the problem, the live demo and what you would do next.
简要回答：先读清楚比赛规则（参赛资格、队伍人数、开赛前能准备什么、评分标准），带着一个问题和队友去，而不是带着做好的代码。比赛中只做一条能跑通的演示路径，随手写 README，最后几小时留给路演：讲清问题、现场演示、下一步计划。

Before the event

  1. Read the rules page carefully. Each event sets its own eligibility, team size and judging criteria. For example, UNIHACK is for current university and TAFE students in Australia and New Zealand, while many company-run hack days are open to anyone who registers. Check whether code written before the event is allowed; many hackathons require projects to be started at the event.
  2. Bring a problem, not a product. Write down two or three problems you understand well, ideally ones that fit the event's theme or sponsor challenges.
  3. Form a balanced team. A typical strong team has someone to build the core, someone for the interface, and someone to own the story and the pitch.
  4. Prepare your tools. Install your stack, sign up for any sponsor APIs or credits the event offers, and test that everything runs on your laptop.

During the event

  1. Scope one demo path. Pick the single flow a judge will see and make it work end to end. Fake anything that isn't on that path.
  2. Check in with mentors early. They often know what judges and sponsors care about.
  3. Keep a README as you go. Problem, how it works, what's real vs mocked, how to run it. Many events ask for a repo or a submission page.
  4. Freeze features early. Leave the last few hours for bug fixes, a recorded backup demo and the pitch.

The pitch

  • Open with who has the problem and why it matters, in one or two sentences.
  • Show the live demo early. Keep a screen recording ready in case the Wi-Fi or an API fails.
  • Map what you show to the judging criteria the organisers published.
  • End with what you would build next and what you need (users, partners, funding).

After the event

Hackathons are a common route into accelerators and grants. Some events send finalists to a bigger stage: for example, finalists of the AI Engineer Sydney Hack Day pitch at the AI Engineer Sydney conference. Keep your demo and README online, and look at our accelerators and grants lists for the next step.

Common questions

Can I start coding before a hackathon?

It depends on the event's rules. Many hackathons require the project to be built during the event and only allow preparation such as ideas, designs and tool setup. Check the rules page of your event.

Do I need a team?

Most hackathons allow solo entries or help you find a team on the day, but a team of three to four with mixed skills is common. Check the event's team size limits.
